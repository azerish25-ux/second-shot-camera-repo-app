# Phase 01 — discovery interview guide

**Status: DRAFTED, NOT USED.** No participant has been interviewed. Use only after
owner approval and the [consent and data handling](consent-and-data-handling.md)
rules are satisfied. Ask about **past behaviour before** showing, describing or
demoing any concept.

## Session shape

- One participant, one interview, 25–30 minutes, sitting at their own workspace.
- Two roles: a facilitator and a separate note-taker. The note-taker records
  participant code, time spent repeating the last shot, tools used, verbatim
  quotes and spend — never a name, address, employer or face photo.
- No recording of the interview, screen, footage or face unless the participant
  gives a separate, explicit yes. Default: notes only, participant code only.
- The concept is shown only in part 4, after parts 1–3 are answered in full.
- The facilitator does not defend the idea, explain the roadmap, sell, or ask for
  a purchase commitment. No prices are quoted; no test build is promised.
- If the participant asks whether Second Shot exists or works, the answer is
  literally true: an early synthetic demo exists, guided retakes are planned and
  not yet proven.

## Part 1 — the last time it mattered (past behaviour only)

Ask in this order. Follow up with "walk me through that" rather than "would you".

1. Tell me about the most recent shot you had to repeat. What exactly did you
   have to get back to?
2. How did you know you had drifted from the original?
3. What did you do next — retake, fix in edit, or change the shot? Walk me
   through the steps in order.
4. How long from "I need that shot again" to "I have something usable"? Roughly
   how much of that was camera work versus editing?
5. How many attempts did that take, including ones you deleted?
6. What went wrong most often? (framing? distance? height? angle? light change?)
7. When it still was not good enough, what did you ship instead?

Follow-ups if the story is thin: the last three repeated shots, whether the
subject moved, whether the tripod or gimbal was already out, whether another
person was involved.

## Part 2 — timeframe, cost and workaround

8. How often does this happen — per project, per week, per month?
9. What do you spend on it today? Split it honestly: time, gear, other people,
   software subscriptions.
10. Which tools did you actually use in the last month, and which were you
    billed for but did not open? (Identifies real spend, not aspirational spend.)
11. What improvised workarounds do you use — tape marks, stand-ins, notes,
    a second take of the whole scene, overshooting and cropping later?
12. What did you try before that you stopped using? Why did it stop?

## Part 3 — constraints, rejection, and the honest no

13. What is the fastest you would accept in this workflow, and what would make
    it too slow to use on a real job?
14. What would make you distrust a tool like this? (Tracking that silently
    drifts, looks precise but is not, hides an error, or needs both hands.)
15. On a real shoot, what is the device situation — locked, gimbal, cage,
    case, one hand free?
16. Where do your clips live, and what are you not willing to upload?
17. What would have to be true for you to **not** adopt this — what would you
    rather keep doing manually?
18. Who would you have to convince for this to become part of your workflow?

Push for rejection reasons here. A participant with no objection is almost always
being polite; ask "what would your past self criticise about it".

## Part 4 — concept reaction (only after 1–3)

19. Show the current synthetic demo, labelled as synthetic, with no claims about
    real camera performance. Then ask: what do you think this does?
20. What part of your last repeated shot would this have helped with, if any?
21. What part would it not have helped with?
22. If it worked exactly as described, what would you stop paying for or
    cancel? What would you not cancel even then?

## Part 5 — willingness to pay (separate genuine intent from courtesy)

23. What did you pay for in the last twelve months in this area, and roughly how
    much? (Prior behaviour beats stated intent.)
24. Which of those purchases did you regret, and why?
25. At what point in your workflow would you decide to pay — before the shoot,
    after a failed retake, or while editing?
26. If a paid tier existed but a free version covered framing only, which one
    would you personally use next week?
27. What would you need to see working, on your own scene, before paying?

Coding for the write-up: mark each answer **observed behaviour**, **current
spend**, **stated intent** or **opinion**. Only the first two support a demand
argument; stated intent is reported as intent with the wording attached. Record
answers that contradict the concept prominently — they are the useful result.

## Analysis and restrictions

- Summarize by participant code with verbatim quotes, then aggregate themes and
  keep every dissenting case visible.
- Do not present interviews as demand. They generate hypotheses and the
  competitor/baseline list for the [retake usefulness protocol](phase-01-protocol.md).
- Interview data does not replace the paired-shot experiment, and it cannot pass
  G1 or G2.
- Contact details, recordings and any personal media stay outside this repository
  and are deleted on the recorded date.

## Banned in this phase

- Selling, pricing promises, income or revenue claims, comparisons with named
  competitors' internal performance.
- Asking a participant to sign anything beyond the consent form.
- Inviting anyone to a beta, TestFlight build or public channel; that needs
  signing authority and separate owner approval.
- Using a participant's footage in marketing without a separate, specific yes.
